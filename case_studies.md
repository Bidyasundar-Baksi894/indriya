## Case Study 1: The Indian SOC Crisis- 95% Burnout & Alert Fatigue

> **The Reality:** India is facing a massive cybersecurity talent shortage (approx. 1.5 million professionals missing), forcing existing Security Operations Center (SOC) teams to handle unsustainable workloads.

**The Breaking Point:**
- In 2025, a staggering **95% of organizations in India reported experiencing cybersecurity burnout** (up from 83% in 2024).
- Indian SOC teams are flooded with over 1,000 alerts per day per analyst.
- This leads directly to the **"cry wolf" effect (Alert Fatigue)**: Analysts become completely desensitized to notifications, auto-resolving or ignoring alerts to clear their queue.

**Why it matters for Indriya:**
When an exhausted Indian analyst ignores a critical alert at 4:30 PM on a Friday (a behavioral error), the company gets breached (like the catastrophic AIIMS ransomware attack). Indriya is the *only* tool that monitors this human fatigue level in real-time and forces a "cognitive break" before the breach happens.

## Case Study 2: RBI & SEBI Regulations - The "Human-in-the-Loop" Mandate

> **The Reality:** Indian financial regulators are cracking down on "black box" AI. You can no longer blame the algorithm.

**RBI's FREE-AI Framework (Aug 2025):**
The Reserve Bank of India mandates that for lending, credit scoring, and risk, **final decision-making must rest with humans.** Banks are legally accountable for the outcomes.

**SEBI's AI Guidelines for Capital Markets:**
SEBI requires human oversight for AI trading tools and mandates **"Kill Switches"** to halt automated systems. Most importantly, SEBI states that "the model decided it" is an unacceptable defense for market manipulation or faulty trades.

**Why it matters for Indriya:**
Banks and brokerages *have* to put a human in the loop. But if that human is suffering from **Automation Bias** — just blindly clicking "Approve" on the AI's credit rejection — the bank is still liable. Indriya proves to RBI/SEBI auditors that the human was *actually thinking*, not just rubber-stamping the AI.

## Case Study 3: The DPDP Act & Algorithmic Accountability

> **The Reality:** The Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023, enforces strict accountability for how personal data is processed.

While the DPDP Act doesn't specifically ban AI, it holds the Data Fiduciary (the company) entirely responsible for any automated processing. If an AI system denies a loan or filters a resume based on biased data, and the human operator fails to catch it because of automation bias, the company faces massive fines (up to ₹250 Crores).

**Why it matters for Indriya:**
Indriya is a compliance tool. It provides the audit trail proving that Indian enterprises are maintaining "meaningful human control" over personal data processing, satisfying DPDP accountability requirements.

## Case Study 4: Indian Enterprise AI — Pilot Purgatory

> **The Reality:** Up to 95% of enterprise AI projects fail globally, and India is suffering from "Pilot Purgatory."

**The Indian Context:**
Indian IT giants and enterprises are buying "Copilots" and GenAI tools rapidly ("License-First Deployments"). However, adoption is failing because they treat it as a software installation, not a behavioral change.

**The "Automation Bias" Trap:**
In Indian BPOs, KPOs, and back-offices, workers are pressured for high throughput. When given an AI tool, they immediately defer to the AI's output to save time (Automation Bias). They stop verifying. Quality plummets, and the AI project is abandoned.

**Why it matters for Indriya:**
Indriya measures the exact moment a worker shifts from "using AI as a tool" to "blindly trusting the AI." It allows Indian enterprises to scale AI adoption safely without sacrificing quality control.

## Case Study 5: Financial Services — The Call Center "Speed" Paradox

> **The Reality:** Indian financial services rely heavily on massive customer support and loan processing centers.

**The Scenario:**
An AI tool suggests a resolution for a customer's fraud claim. The agent, under pressure to meet Average Handling Time (AHT) metrics, clicks "Approve" in 0.8 seconds. It is physically impossible for the agent to have read the AI's reasoning in 0.8 seconds. 

**Why it matters for Indriya:**
Indriya's **Velocity Heatmap** catches this immediately. The **Speed Gate** guardrail intercepts the 0.8-second click and forces the agent to type: *"Why are you approving this fraud claim?"* It injects "cognitive friction" exactly where the process is broken.
